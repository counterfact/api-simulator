# Working hypothesis and interpretation

Formulated September 9, 2026 after inspecting the observations. This is a retrospective engineering assessment, not a preregistered experiment. It does not alter the census protocol, classifications, fixed experiment population, or underlying observations.

Working hypothesis: Agent-assisted development can increase delivery while preserving a working verification and repair loop.

Observable checks:

1. Compare non-dependency merge counts in the same calendar window across 2022–2026. A count not exceeding the highest observed earlier matched-window count would count against the delivery expectation. The 2022 startup history begins April 8. Counts do not measure effort or value; released capabilities provide concrete context.
2. Trace all confirmed external product-defect reports after adoption to public corrections and report the time from report to corrected publication. A report in the fixed seven-case population with no traceable public correction by the study boundary would count against the repair-loop expectation. The separate ninety-day follow-up used for the supporting historical analysis is only an observation and censoring rule, not a repair target or MTTR measure.
3. Run the same relevant behavioral assertion against affected and corrected historical states for all seven cases. Non-discriminating or inconclusive results would fail to establish the verification expectation for that case. These checks are retrospective, mostly reuse tests added with the repairs, and do not demonstrate preventive detection.

The first check supports a temporal change in activity. The second and third test whether specific feedback loops were demonstrably functioning, not whether they improved relative to an equivalently measured pre-adoption baseline. Together they inform the maintainer’s decision to continue the approach. They do not identify a causal AI effect, establish unchanged reliability, or measure review burden.

Counterevidence remains in view: a released post-adoption middleware regression, the release-provenance incident, unknown discovery channels, unresolved released status, and incomplete ascertainment. Future accumulated reports without corrections or checks that cannot detect their target failures would weaken the assessment.
